DeepInfer Unit Economics

DeepSeek's open inference engine ships with three throughput charts and no cost figures. This page turns those charts into dollars per token, sets them against today's V4.1-Flash prices and real OpenRouter traffic, and checks a widely shared margin estimate. GPU price defaults to Silicon Data's B200 rental index; utilization is an assumption you can change. Throughput is read off the charts in the repo.

DeepSeek's realised price: OpenRouter reports an effective output price of $0.936/1M for DeepSeek's own V4.1-Flash 1017 endpoint. Output is never cached, so that is 0.60 × (1 + peak share), which puts 56% of its output tokens in peak hours that cover only 21% of the week. Checking the “87.76% to 96.94%” tweet

@zephyr_z9, 20 Oct 2026. Assumes 97% cached input, 2% uncached input and 1% output; 80k uncached input tok/s/GPU for prefill and 16k output tok/s/GPU at 250 tok/s/user for decode; $3–6 per GPU-hour; DeepSeek's off-peak and peak list prices. Result: 87.76%–93.88% margin off-peak, 93.88%–96.94% at peak.

The arithmetic reproduces exactly (875 GPU-seconds and $11.91 of off-peak revenue per billion tokens). The throughput inputs match DeepInfer's charts: 16k at 250 tok/s/user is the EP64, 64-request point. The rows below keep the tweet's throughput and change one assumption at a time.

  • The mix assumption is wrong but doesn't matter much. Real V4.1-Flash traffic on OpenRouter has twice the output share and four times the uncached input of the tweet's mix. Revenue rises with it, because DeepSeek prices each token type at a similar multiple of its GPU cost (about 14× for uncached input and 12× for output at $3/hr). Margin barely moves.
  • Utilization and price matter most. Benchmark throughput assumes the GPUs are busy every second. At a Copilot-shaped 53% utilization, the off-peak margin at $6/hr falls to about 74%. At the prices resellers actually charge, the margin falls further.
  • This is not DeepSeek's own margin. It describes a Blackwell operator charging DeepSeek's list price. DeepSeek can't buy SM100. Its actual fleet cost is unknown, and its reported API gross margin was 82.9% (blended with the free app: 44.6%).
  • GPU rental is not all of cost of goods. KV-cache storage for 92–97% hit rates, networking, CPUs and peak-hour headroom all sit outside the GPU-hour.

DeepInfer's benchmarks are SM100 chips (B200 and GB200). B300 is faster, so its price overstates cost at these throughputs. Utilization presets

A fleet is sized for its busiest hour, so outside that hour some GPUs sit idle unless other work fills them. If the fleet is just full at peak, average utilization = 1 ÷ (peak-to-average demand). None of these figures is a measured V4.1-Flash fleet utilization; nobody publishes one.

All three assume GPUs run at benchmark throughput whenever they are busy. Real batches run below that (variable prompt lengths, KV-cache fragmentation, draft acceptance under 75%), and a provider can’t run at 100% even at peak without queuing. Both push the real figure lower.

Why these are best-case numbers

  • Draft acceptance is fixed at 75%. The sweep assumes 3.25 tokens per decode step. Real acceptance depends on content, and output cost moves with it almost one for one.
  • Lab runs are steady state. A production fleet has idle hours and must provision for peaks. DeepSeek's peak-hour share of output (56%) is far above the share of hours that are peak (21%).
  • Several components are missing. The release leaves out separate prefill and decode clusters, the KV cache system and load balancing across replicas.
  • Cheap resellers often quantize. The lowest prices on OpenRouter are FP4 endpoints; DeepInfer's charts are for DeepSeek's own precision.
  • Throughput values are read from chart images and are accurate to roughly ±2%.
